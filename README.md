# Outreach — LinkedIn Content Engine

Purpose: produce LinkedIn posts in Ibrahim Elhofy's voice. Persona and voice rules
live in the profile SOUL.md — this file is the genre playbook.

## How to use this file

1. Pick a genre.
2. Get the raw material from Ibrahim (story, news, concept, opinion).
3. Apply the genre's **Secret Sauce** below — structure, pacing, what to include,
   what to cut.
4. Write in Ibrahim's voice (see SOUL.md), using the genre's structure — never
   copy phrasing from references/.
5. Ibrahim reviews; iterate until it sounds like him.

## Genre 1: Experience (story → lesson)

**Trigger:** Ibrahim has a real story from his career — a failure, a win, a
moment of humiliation, a lesson learned the hard way.

### Secret Sauce

- **Open with a scene, not a summary.** Drop the reader into a specific moment:
  a phone call, a bug he couldn't find, a client who betrayed him. Never start
  with "في هذا البوست" or "خليني أحكيلك قصة."
- **Real stakes = real engagement.** Money lost, ego bruised, time wasted,
  sleepless nights. The more specific the pain, the more the reader feels it.
- **Reconstructed dialogue is the engine.** "قالهالي في أول ساعة... وأنا رديت
  عليه في ثانيتين." Make the reader hear the conversation.
- **The turn is a moment of irony or realization.** The lesson is not stated
  upfront — it's earned by the story. The pivot is usually "بس" or "المفارقة"
  or a sharp rhetorical question.
- **The lesson is often paradoxical.** "الخبرة بتخليك أسرع في استبعاد
  الاحتمالات... لحد ما تستبعد الاحتمال الصح." Not a platitude — a real
  contradiction he lived.
- **End with a direct question to the reader.** Often addresses two audiences:
  "لو انت senior... ولو لسه في الأول..." Make them pick a side.
- **Numbers and timeframes are non-negotiable.** "يومين" "أول ساعة" "15 سنة."
  Vague time = vague story = no engagement.

### What to cut

- General advice without a story
- Lessons that could apply to anyone (if it's not specific to his experience, it
  doesn't belong here)
- Multiple stories in one post — one story, one lesson

## Genre 2: News (new thing → practical take → question)

**Trigger:** A new tool, model, vulnerability, or shift in the tech landscape
that Ibrahim thinks his audience should know about.

### Secret Sauce

- **Open with a bold, surprising claim or fact.** Not "X released Y." Instead:
  "تطبيق عادي بقى root على OnePlus 15" or "السعر نزل والأداء طلع في نفس
  السطر." Stop the scroll with the implication, not the announcement.
- **The news is a hook, not the point.** The point is what it means for the
  reader. Always answer "so what?" within the first few lines.
- **Include a "but" or "the real story is" pivot.** The surface news is never
  the interesting part. "بس الحتة اللي مش باينة في العنوان هي الـ cache."
- **Numbers are specific and concrete.** Prices, percentages, timeframes,
  version numbers. "66.4% قدام 55.8%" not "أداء أحسن."
- **The turn is "here's what this means for you."** Practical implication,
  not technical deep-dive. The reader should know what to do with this info.
- **End with a direct, specific question.** Not "إيه رأيكم؟" but "فاتورتك الشهر
  اللي فات كانت cache reads كام في المية منها؟" Make them check their own
  situation.
- **Links and resources go in the first comment.** Keep the post clean.
- **Tone: "I found this interesting and here's why you should care."** Not
  hype, not fear — informed curiosity with a practical edge.

### What to cut

- Benchmark numbers without context (what does the score mean for the reader?)
- Technical deep-dives that don't serve the "so what"
- Multiple news items in one post — one news, one take

## Genre 3: Tutorial (clear explainer)

**Trigger:** A concept that people get wrong or confuse — often something
Ibrahim was asked about in an interview or by a junior dev.

### Secret Sauce

- **Open with a relatable pain point or question.** Often in dialogue form:
  "يا هندسة، جالي سؤال في انترفيو..." Make the reader feel the confusion.
- **Each concept gets a concrete, visual scenario.** Not abstract definitions.
  "تخيل صفحة Product في E-commerce محتاجة السعر من Pricing Service..." The
  reader should see the code in their head.
- **Use comparisons and contrasts heavily.** WhenAll vs WhenAny vs WaitAll —
  the differences become clear when they're side by side.
- **Structure: scenario → what happens → when to use it.** Not "here's the
  definition." The reader should know when to reach for each tool.
- **End with a quick-reference summary.** A table, a bullet list, a one-liner
  per concept. Make it saveable.
- **Tone: "let me explain this simply."** Not academic, not condescending.
  The reader should feel smarter after reading, not smaller.
- **Bullet points and clear formatting are your friend.** This is the most
  scannable genre — make it easy to skim.

### What to cut

- Abstract definitions without examples
- Edge cases that confuse more than clarify
- More than 4-5 concepts per post — if it's too much, split it

## Genre 4: Wisdoms (distilled opinion/theory)

**Trigger:** A strong opinion or observation about the industry, career, or
life — often contrarian, always earned through experience.

### Secret Sauce

- **Open with a bold, contrarian statement.** "من أكثر الأفكار استفزازاً
  للاستشاري أن تقيس قيمة عمله بعدد الساعات." Stop the scroll with a
   statement the reader disagrees with or has never considered.
- **The tone is more formal than other genres.** Closer to fusha, more
  "wise elder" than "friend sharing a story." Still Ibrahim — still Egyptian
  — but more measured.
- **Paradoxes and contrasts are the engine.** "الحشو رخيص، الاختصار مكلف."
  "مش X، ده Y." The wisdom lives in the contradiction.
- **Structure: observation → elaboration → lesson.** Not a story — an
  argument. The reader should feel like they're hearing a principle, not
  an anecdote.
- **Sometimes includes numbered points or lists.** "3 أخطاء قاتلة..." —
  but only when the list earns its place.
- **The lesson is universal.** Not tied to a specific story — it should
  apply to anyone in the reader's position.
- **Can include religious or cultural references naturally.** "والله
  المستعان" — not as decoration, but as part of the worldview.
- **End with a philosophical or motivational note.** Not a question —
  a statement to sit with. Or a CTA if it's tied to a service.
- **The voice is "I've thought about this for years and here's what I've
  learned."** Not preachy — earned.

### What to cut

- Stories that belong in the Experience genre (if it's a specific incident,
  it's not a wisdom)
- Generic advice that could come from anyone
- More than one wisdom per post — one idea, fully explored
